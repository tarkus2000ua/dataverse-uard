About the repository

The National Research Data Repository (UARD) is being developed by the State Scientific and Technical Library of Ukraine (SSTL of Ukraine) on the open-source Dataverse platform. The platform provides research data management and supports open data exchange standards, an application programming interface (API), the OAI-PMH protocol, international metadata standards and the FAIR principles.

Project

The project is carried out as part of the implementation of state policy in the field of open science and of the measures under the 2026–2027 Action Plan for the Implementation of the Open Government Partnership Initiative, approved by Order of the Cabinet of Ministers of Ukraine No. 603-r of 17 June 2026. It is also carried out within the research project “Study of International Experience in Research Data Management and Development of the Methodological Framework for the Establishment of the National Repository of Scientific Data” (state registration number 0125U001107).

Purpose

The UARD  operates in order to:

  • support the principles of open science and ensure the reproducibility of research results on the basis of publicly available, verified research data;
  • help UARD  stakeholders comply with state policy on research data management and with international standards and the FAIR principles in their research activities;
  • improve access to research data in order to raise the quality of research and the efficiency of public spending, in particular by avoiding duplicate research and experiments;
  • facilitate the integration of Ukraine’s research sector into the European Research Area, the European Open Science Cloud (EOSC) and the international research community by harmonising standards for research data management and exchange.

Objectives

The objectives of the UARD are to:

  • collect and store datasets deposited by researchers and higher education institutions of Ukraine, and ensure their integrity;
  • assign a unique digital identifier to each research dataset;
  • create reliable, up-to-date and structured metadata for research datasets in line with international standards to ensure their findability and interoperability;
  • ensure the integration of the UARD  with external national and international research information systems;
  • enable deposited datasets to be linked, through a system of persistent identifiers, with data management plans, research publications, software code, projects and funding sources;
  • protect information and users’ personal data in accordance with the legislation of Ukraine;
  • implement the principles of open science and the FAIR standards in the activities of UARD  stakeholders;
  • ensure the long-term preservation and accessibility of research data regardless of changes in technology platforms, file formats or the organisational structure of institutions.

Operating principles

The NRDR operates in accordance with the following principles:

  • Openness and public availability: data and metadata published in the UARD  in open access are available free of charge and without any discrimination to all categories of users, under the terms of the applicable licence.
  • Machine-readability: dataset metadata and, where possible, data files are provided in machine-readable formats that enable automated processing, exchange and reuse.
  • Neutrality and non-discrimination: the UARD  does not give preference to any research institution, research field, user category or type of research data. The conditions for deposit, access and licensing are the same for all users.
  • Technological neutrality: the UARD  runs on publicly available open-source software and open standards, which rules out dependence on specific software vendors or cloud service providers.
  • Transparency and accountability: usage statistics of the UARD are openly available, annual reports on its operation are published, and a user feedback mechanism is maintained.
  • The FAIR principles:
    • findability: each research dataset is assigned persistent identifiers and structured metadata that are indexed by the UARD  and by external search engines and aggregators;
    • accessibility: research data and their metadata are accessible via open, standardised and free protocols (HTTP/S, API, OAI-PMH);
    • interoperability: metadata are created in accordance with recognised international standards and controlled vocabularies, and include references to other relevant data, publications and resources using standardised formats such as JSON-LD, XML, Schema.org and DCAT;
    • reusability: each dataset has a defined international Creative Commons licence or terms of use, and detailed metadata including information on the provenance of the data, the methodology used to obtain them and the conditions for reproducing the research.

International standards and integration

The UARD operates in accordance with international standards and best practices in research data management and open science.

To this end:

  • metadata comply with international standards and profiles (DataCite Metadata Schema, Dublin Core, Schema.org, DCAT, and the OpenAIRE Guidelines for Data Archives);
  • the UARD  undergoes certification as a trustworthy repository under the internationally recognised CoreTrustSeal scheme and maintains this certification;
  • the UARD  is registered in the international Registry of Research Data Repositories (re3data), as well as in the relevant national registries, and its information there is kept up to date;
  • metadata are integrated and exchanged in machine-readable form with external systems via open protocols (OAI-PMH, REST API) and persistent identifier systems (ORCID, DOI, ROR);
  • ethical principles of data management are applied to research data concerning human subjects or vulnerable groups, or with other specific characteristics; personal data must be anonymised, and data protection legislation must be complied with.
